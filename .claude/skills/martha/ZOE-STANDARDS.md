# ZOE standards — the filter every recipe must pass

Used when `martha-config.md` sets the nutrition standard to `zoe`. ZOE
([zoe.com](https://zoe.com)) is a nutrition science company whose gut-health guidance this file
follows: 30 different plants a week, fiber first, and as little ultra-processed food as possible.
Sourced from ZOE's own published guidance, and applied on top of each member's restrictions in the
config.

## The five rules that decide yes/no

A recipe clears the bar if it does all five:

1. **Contributes distinct plants.** The target is the config's weekly plant target (ZOE's is
   **30 different plant foods per week**) across the whole slate. Count vegetables, fruits,
   legumes, whole grains, nuts, seeds, herbs and spices: each distinct species counts once, no
   matter how often it appears. Bonus plants: coffee, tea, extra virgin olive oil, dark chocolate
   ≥70%. Different colors of the same plant (red vs green grapes) do **not** count twice.
2. **Fiber-forward.** Legumes, whole grains, nuts, seeds and vegetables carry the meal. A recipe
   whose bulk is refined starch fails, gluten-free or not.
3. **No naked carbs.** Every carbohydrate is paired with fat, protein or fiber in the same dish.
   This is the most common reason a gluten-free recipe fails ZOE: GF pasta or rice alone is a
   spike.
4. **Whole-food base, minimal UPF.** Built from recognizable ingredients. See the UPF tiers below.
5. **No added sugar or artificial sweeteners** in adult-facing items. Sweetness comes from whole
   fruit.

## Plant-count tracking

Keep a running list of distinct plants across the salad, the dinners, the breakfast and the snack
line. Report the tally when proposing the slate (it is a gate: `SKILL.md` Step 5). If the week
lands under target, the fix is almost always a wider herb and spice hand, a second legume, seeds
on the salad, or a third berry.

**Frozen and pre-cut count exactly the same as whole.** Frozen vegetables are picked and frozen
at ripeness and often carry more nutrition than tired fresh; bagged shredded cabbage is the same
plant as a whole cabbage; jarred minced garlic is still garlic. The prepped-first preference in
`PREPPED-FIRST.md` costs nothing against ZOE and should never be argued down on nutrition grounds.

Cheap plant-count wins: chia, flax, pumpkin seeds, walnuts, almonds, lentils, chickpeas, black
beans, quinoa, buckwheat, oats (certified gluten-free where a member needs it), cumin, turmeric,
paprika, cinnamon, oregano, basil, cilantro, garlic, onion, ginger, extra virgin olive oil.

## Prioritize, actively

- **Fermented foods** for live cultures: sauerkraut, kimchi, coconut yogurt with live cultures,
  miso, water kefir, kombucha. For a dairy-free member, dairy-free routes only: **never** yogurt,
  kefir or aged cheese. For a gluten-free member, rice or chickpea miso (many miso pastes contain
  barley).
- **Polyphenols**: deeply colored produce, berries, red onion, extra virgin olive oil, herbs,
  spices, dark chocolate ≥70%, coffee, green tea.
- **Prebiotics**: bananas, blueberries, oats, garlic, onions, leeks, asparagus, legumes.
- **Omega-3**: walnuts, chia, flax, and oily fish (salmon, sardines, mackerel). Watch the price;
  frozen salmon on promo is usually the way in.
- **Legumes in most dinners.** The cheapest fiber and plant-count lever on the shelf, and they
  scale a recipe up without scaling the cost.

## Limit or avoid

- **Worst UPF tier, keep out of the cart:** processed meats, sugar-sweetened beverages,
  artificially sweetened drinks, ready-to-eat/ready-to-heat mixed dishes, and most jarred sauces,
  spreads and condiments.
- **Acceptable UPF tier, fine in moderation:** whole-grain bread, plain cold cereals, plant-based
  alternatives, popcorn and similar savory snacks, fruit-based products. This is where the adult
  snack allowance should live.
- **Added sugar and artificial sweeteners.** Read the label: "no sugar added" on the front does
  not mean no sucralose on the back.
- **Late-night eating and constant grazing.** Snacks are portioned into the day, not open-ended.

Label red flags, in ZOE's framing: high energy density, engineered hyperpalatability, and
non-culinary additives (artificial colors, sweeteners, preservatives).

**Settled exceptions.** Items on the config's settled-exceptions list override this section: they
go in every order and are never flagged (`QUANTITY-MATH.md`). These rules govern the recipes and
the adult-snack selection, not those items.

**Members who follow it to different degrees.** The config marks each member strict, loose or
not following. When a judgment call comes up on shared adult food, hold the line for the
strictest member, and don't police a looser member's settled exceptions.

## Blood-sugar tactics to bake into the plan

These belong in the plan's Prep section, not just in your head:

- **Eat in order:** vegetables first, protein and fat second, starch and sugar last. Up to ~75%
  smaller glucose response from the same food.
- **Veg starter.** A small vegetable course before dinner is the easiest win. If you plan one, it
  needs its own greens: the mass-prep salad is sized for lunches only, and a dinner starter is up
  to another full batch. Either add a separate bag of greens to the cart or skip the starter. Do
  not plan one batch to cover both.
- **A tablespoon of vinegar** before a starchy meal cuts the spike ~30%. Build acid into the salad
  dressing by default.
- **Savory breakfast beats sweet.** The 7-day breakfast should be protein- and fat-forward.
  Overnight oats need nuts, seeds and a protein source, not fruit and oats alone.
- **Ten minutes of movement after meals.** Worth one line in the plan.

## Gluten-free crossovers that trip people up

When anyone eating a dish is gluten-free, check these every time, because the GF version is a
different product:

- Soy sauce → **tamari** or GF soy sauce
- Oats → **certified gluten-free** oats
- Miso → **rice or chickpea miso**, not barley
- Broth and stock → certified GF, and no sugar added
- Curry paste, gochujang, hoisin, teriyaki, Worcestershire → almost always contain wheat
- Oat milk, malt vinegar, seitan, couscous, farro, bulgur, orzo → not GF
- Baking powder, spice blends, imitation crab, canned soups → hidden wheat

## Dairy-free crossovers

When anyone eating a dish is dairy-free:

- Butter → extra virgin olive oil or avocado oil
- Cream → full-fat coconut milk or cashew cream
- Parmesan → nutritional yeast
- Yogurt → coconut yogurt with live cultures
- Ghee, casein, whey, "natural flavors" in seasoning blends → check the label
- Milk → an unsweetened plant milk (the config's standing order names the household's)

## Young children

A child eats the same food at the portion fraction in the config, within their own restrictions
(a child may be allowed dairy the adults avoid). Practical notes:

- Keep a portion of each dinner unspiced or mildly spiced before the heat goes in.
- Cut to toddler-safe shapes; nothing round and firm whole (grapes, cherry tomatoes, whole nuts).
- Nut butters thin-spread, not spooned.
- A child-only item (cheese sticks, say) belongs in the standing order with that child's name on
  it.
