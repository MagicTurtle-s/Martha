# Prepped-first — buy the vegetable already cut

**Standing preference: always choose the pre-cut, pre-shredded, frozen or pre-minced form of an
ingredient unless it would make the dish worse.** The cook's time is the scarce resource, not the
knife work. This applies at recipe selection (Step 5) and at the cart or shopping list (Step 8).
It is on by default; `martha-config.md` can switch it off, and its never-whole list names items
that are always bought prepped, even where the list below would allow whole.

Two payoffs: it protects the time ceiling at batch scale, and it lets recipes be more ambitious
than a from-scratch prep budget would allow. A recipe that fails the ceiling with whole
vegetables often passes comfortably with prepped ones, so check the prepped version before
rejecting a recipe on time.

## The hierarchy

For each ingredient, walk down this list and take the first form that doesn't hurt the dish:

1. **Pre-cut fresh**: bagged shredded, pre-sliced, pre-diced from the produce case
2. **Frozen pre-cut**: diced, chopped, florets, cubes
3. **Paste, jar or frozen cube**: for aromatics
4. **Whole, cut it yourself**: only when 1–3 would degrade the result

## Aromatics — always the shortcut

Never write "mince 3 cloves of garlic" into a plan when these exist:

| Instead of | Buy |
|---|---|
| Fresh garlic cloves | Jarred minced garlic, garlic paste, or frozen garlic cubes |
| Fresh ginger root | Ginger paste, jarred minced ginger, or frozen ginger cubes |
| Fresh onion (for cooked dishes) | Frozen chopped onion, or pre-diced from the produce case |
| Mirepoix base | Frozen onion-celery-carrot blend |
| Lemongrass, turmeric root | Paste or frozen cube form |
| Chopped herbs stirred into a cooked dish | Frozen herb cubes |

Conversion, for scaling: 1 clove garlic ≈ ½ tsp jarred minced. 1 tsp fresh grated ginger ≈ 1 tsp
paste. Write the converted amount into the plan, not the original.

## Frozen is fine here

Anything that ends up cooked. Frozen is picked at ripeness and often beats tired fresh:

corn · peas · edamame · green beans · chopped spinach · chopped kale · broccoli and cauliflower
florets · diced butternut squash · diced sweet potato · chopped onion · chopped bell pepper ·
stir-fry blends · mirepoix · diced carrot · artichoke hearts · berries for baked goods and smoothies

## Pre-cut fresh is the right call here

Cases where you want prepped but *not* frozen: frozen-then-thawed goes limp, so the bagged fresh
form is the correct shortcut.

| Ingredient | Buy this |
|---|---|
| Cabbage for a cold salad | Bagged shredded cabbage or coleslaw mix |
| Carrot for a cold salad | Bagged shredded or matchstick carrots |
| Kale for a cold salad | Bagged chopped kale |
| Onion for a cold salad | Pre-diced from the produce case |
| Mushrooms | Pre-sliced fresh (frozen mushrooms are watery) |
| Brussels sprouts | Bagged shaved sprouts |
| Butternut squash for roasting | Pre-cut fresh cubes if available, frozen if not |
| Salad greens | Bagged, undressed |

If the store doesn't carry the pre-cut form (the deals sweep shows what it has; in plan-only
mode, list the pre-cut form with "or whole"), buy whole and say so; record it in the config's store notes so next week doesn't search again.

## Keep it fresh and whole — these get worse prepped

Don't apply the rule here. This is the short list, and it's short on purpose:

- **Avocado**: always fresh, always cut at serving.
- **Tomatoes eaten raw**: grape or cherry, fresh. Frozen tomato is mush; fine in a sauce, wrong in
  a bowl or salad.
- **Cucumber, raw**: fresh.
- **Finishing herbs**: cilantro or parsley scattered on top at serving wants to be fresh. Frozen
  is fine when it cooks into the dish.
- **Vegetables that need to brown or crisp**: asparagus, green beans, and thin vegetables for a
  sheet pan or air fryer. Frozen releases water and steams instead of roasting. Whole potatoes
  roast better than frozen for the same reason, though frozen roasting-cut potatoes are
  acceptable.
- **Anything that must stay crunchy on day 5** of the mass-prep salad. Bagged fresh is the
  shortcut here, not frozen.
- **Citrus for a dressing**: fresh juice is better. Bottled is an acceptable trade if it's
  discounted and the dressing is one component among many; say which you chose.

The config's never-whole items override this list.

## Cost interaction

Prepped forms sometimes carry a premium. The preference still holds:

- Premium under about **$1.50 per item**: just buy the prepped form, no comment needed.
- Premium over that: buy it anyway, but note it at hand-off (Step 9) so the household can
  overrule.
- If prepped forms push the order past budget, cut from `QUANTITY-MATH.md`'s ranked list. **Do
  not revert to whole vegetables to save money**: that trades the cook's time for a couple of
  dollars, which is the wrong direction.

## What to say in the proposal

In Step 5, state the prepped form in the ingredient line and let the time estimate reflect it:
"bagged shredded cabbage and matchstick carrots, so the batch is 15 minutes, not 30." If a recipe
only clears the ceiling *because* of prepped ingredients, say that too, so the plan isn't quietly
fragile if the household ends up with whole vegetables.

Frozen and pre-cut vegetables count toward a plant target exactly the same as whole ones. See
`ZOE-STANDARDS.md`: this preference costs nothing nutritionally.
